This repository is a companion simulation to a paper that extends the school choice model by including transport routes into preference lists.
The simulation uses real data found in "C:\Users\aejp1u19\geo-model\data."

load_data.py reads in the geospacial and statistical data from the data folder and merges them into dataframes, for the local authorities (LAs) named, as the school register's "LA (name)" spells them (Southampton by default). An LSOA belongs to the LA its 2024 district (from the IoD2025 files) falls under: a two-tier district (E07) to its county, read from the ONS district-to-county lookup in data/student_data, every other district being an LA of its own. The LA's name is linked to that code by the register's GSSLACode. Schools are those the LA runs, a secondary school's name taking its LA in brackets where two of the LAs named share it. A secondary school's seats, and the fair share route seats are sized on, are the floor of the mean of the Year 7 places it offered over the latest five admission years, 2022/23 to 2026/27 (`OFFER_YEARS`), from DfE's school-level applications and offers file (data/school_data/school_applications_and_offers/supporting-files). Places offered are the offers made on national offer day, so they are capped by demand and fall below a published admission number at an undersubscribed school. A secondary school that offered no Year 7 place in those years, such as one admitting at 14 or into Year 9, is printed and dropped from the choice set, as one without a Progress 8 score is. Primary schools are sized on the register's capacity spread over the years they span. Every LSOA's cohorts of four- and eleven-year-olds are read from the ONS estimates of mid-2011 to mid-2024 in data/student_data/LSOA_PopEstimates and summarised as their mean and sample standard deviation over the years, each year weighted equally; a year two workbooks hold (mid-2022) is read from the later release, which revises it.

Every entry point takes `--la NAME [NAME ...]` and `--merge`. Without `--merge` each LA named is simulated on its own, one after another; with it, LAs named that share a stretch of boundary (directly or through a chain of named neighbours) are simulated as one region. Each region writes to output/<LA names joined by "+" in alphabetical order>/, e.g. output/Southampton/ or output/Hampshire+Southampton/, so runs for different regions can run at the same time without overwriting each other. temp/ holds only the population cache, shared by every region.

build_prefs.py samples points (students) from a bivariate normal distribution centred on the population centroids for each LSOA and truncated by the LSOA boundary. How many it samples in an LSOA is drawn afresh for every seed from a normal of the cohort's mean and standard deviation over the years, rounded to the nearest student, a negative draw placing none. It uses the distance between students and schools to build their preferences and priorities, respectively. Of the students sampled in each LSOA, round(IDACI score × cohort) are drawn disadvantaged at random, the IDACI score (data/student_data/IoD2025/File_5) being the share of the LSOA's children in income-deprived families, and disadvantaged students rank with their own performance weight and route discount. Routes leave only the LSOAs at or below the IDACI decile. The routes into a school hold k × its places × D / N seats between them, D being the region's disadvantaged students and N its cohort, both taken on the mean cohort so one route set serves every seed, split between the school's routes by largest remainder in proportion to the disadvantaged students of each route's district, weighted by the progressivity.

`DISADVANTAGE` in build_routes.py, `--disadvantage` on the command line of __main__.py and optimise.py, sets who is disadvantaged and who may ride: `score` (the default) draws the group from IDACI scores and routes carry only its students; `score-open` draws the group the same way but routes carry every student of their district; `decile` makes every student of an LSOA at or below the decile disadvantaged and able to ride, the model as it was before the scores were drawn from. Under every choice route seats are sized on the disadvantaged students. Each seed draws its cohorts, then the students' locations, then the group, so every choice matches the same students.

matching.py contains the Deferred Acceptance with Transport (DAT) matching mechanism. It takes student preferences, school priorities, school capacities, and route capacities as input and outputs a stable matching. A school's priority is only ever read for the bundle a student proposes, so `rank_bundles` in utils.py returns each school's rank of every bundle beside it on the student's preference list (students × options) rather than an array over every school, route and student, which for a county or a merged region would run to tens of GB.
matching.md contains more information relevant to the matching paradigm.

dissimilarity.py draws fresh student samples over many seeds, matches each with and without routes, scores every matching with the dissimilarity index and box-plots the index per scenario. Wherever the index with routes is compared with the index without, the two are t-tested: both scenarios of a seed match the same students, so the test is paired by seed (`routes_t_test` in utils.py), and the box plot is titled with it. It is undefined, and said to be, with one seed or where routes change no seed's index.

car_displacement.py matches fresh student samples with and without routes and estimates the travel mode of every seated student from the National Travel Survey's mode shares by trip length (data/travel_data/national_travel_survey), routed students taking their route, then plots the modes of both scenarios and the change between them. The mode estimate itself lives in utils.py and is scored alongside the dissimilarity index, so the sweep carries it too.

__main__.py (`python -m geo_model`) sweeps each model parameter one at a time over the same student samples, scores every setting with and without routes, and plots the index, who the matching leaves unassigned, each school's intake and the change in travel mode against each parameter, singly and as a matrix of subplots. Every value of the index panels is starred by its cell's paired t-test, the p-values Holm-corrected over every cell of the sweep (`holm` in utils.py), and the tests are printed and written to t_tests.csv. By default the intake panels show each school's term of the dissimilarity index (a_c/A − b_c/B); `--intake share` shows the disadvantaged share of each intake instead. `--map` also maps the first sample's matchings at the default settings, with routes and without: LSOAs coloured by IDACI decile, schools by Progress 8, and a line from every seated student to their school, routed students' lines in their own colour. `--lorenz` also plots, with routes and without side by side, the Lorenz curve of disadvantaged against seated students over schools at the default settings, a line per seed, with the Gini coefficient over the seeds.

optimise.py (`python -m geo_model.optimise`) searches the parameters jointly with NSGA-II (pymoo) for the Pareto front of its objectives, by default minimising the dissimilarity index with routes and maximising the students the routes displace from car travel, each the mean over several seeds. Every candidate setting runs the pipeline end to end on the same student samples, as a sweep cell does. By default it searches the route policy levers (min_distance, capacity_scale, progressivity, max_local_p8, local_radius), each over the span GRID sweeps it, holding the rest at DEFAULTS. The decile can be searched, rounded to a whole decile, under every choice of `--disadvantage` but `decile`, where it moves the group the index measures; the other students' route discount only under `score-open`, the one choice where they ride. A setting that leaves no route (`EmptyRouteSet`) is reported and marked infeasible. It then takes the front's compromise setting, the one nearest the ideal once each objective is rescaled to [0, 1] over the front, and compares it with the same students matched without routes on held-out seeds the search never saw (as many as dissimilarity.py draws): every metric the sweep draws (the dissimilarity box plot titled with its paired t-test, which is printed too, the students left unassigned stacked by group, a heatmap of each school's intake beside the register's FSM figure, which `--intake` switches as in the sweep, and the travel-mode plot), the Lorenz curves, the map, and a stacked bar per route of its disadvantaged and other riders below its unused seats (`score_settings` keeps route rows only when asked, with `utilisation`, since a sweep would hold millions), each plot the scripts share drawn by its own script's plotting function. It writes every setting scored, the front, a plot of the front marking the compromise and the defaults, and the comparison plots to output/<region>/optimise; the sweep writes to output/<region>/sweep.

scenario_1.py (`python -m geo_model.scenario_1`) runs one fixed scenario over 100 seeds by default. Routes keep the default minimum distance, 2 miles (`MIN_ROUTE_DISTANCE`), and have no progressivity. The condition on nearby schools is off (`max_local_p8` is infinite). Each school keeps only its shortest route, the one to its nearest route-eligible district beyond the minimum distance holding a seat (`shortest_only` in Settings and route_network). That route's seats are sized as usual, on the default capacity scale unless `--capacity-scale` sets another, and rounded up by default, so it keeps at least one. The script matches the students with and without these routes and draws every plot optimise.py draws of its compromise, with optimise's `compare`. It reports each route's seats and riders, mean over seeds, and writes the route set, the score rows, the per-seed route riders (route_utilisation.csv), each district's disadvantaged and advantaged students with the disadvantaged share of its cohort and its shares of the region's two groups (districts.csv, `district_groups` in utils.py, the same for every seed) and the plots to output/<region>/scenario_1.

utils.py contains all the helper functions used in other scripts.
